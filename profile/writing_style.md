# Anna's Writing Style

## Core voice

Write like an experienced, thoughtful person, not like a resume generator.

The tone should be:

- clear
- natural
- confident
- practical
- professional
- specific
- warm when appropriate
- never exaggerated

## Sentence style

Prefer short and medium-length sentences.

Use plain language.

Lead with the point.

Use concrete actions and outcomes.

Avoid stacking several abstract nouns in one sentence.

Avoid unnecessary adjectives.

## Preferred wording

Prefer:

- built
- improved
- clarified
- coordinated
- investigated
- simplified
- supported
- analyzed
- explained
- worked with
- helped deliver
- translated
- identified
- created
- organized
- resolved

Use strong verbs only when the evidence supports them.

## Avoid

Do not use:

- leveraged
- spearheaded
- championed
- orchestrated
- revolutionized
- game-changing
- dynamic professional
- results-driven
- seasoned professional
- passionate about
- proven track record
- exceptional
- highly accomplished
- visionary
- thought leader
- synergy
- robust
- cutting-edge
- seamless
- transformative
- fast-paced environment
- wear many hats
- go-getter

Avoid phrases that sound generated:

- "I am uniquely positioned to..."
- "My diverse background has equipped me..."
- "I thrive at the intersection of..."
- "I would be thrilled to bring..."
- "This opportunity strongly resonates with me..."
- "Throughout my career..."
- "In today's rapidly evolving landscape..."

## Resume rules

- Keep bullets concise.
- Start with the strongest relevant action.
- Include the outcome when known.
- Do not force metrics.
- Do not repeat the same opening verb too often.
- Do not copy full sentences from the job description.
- Use job-description language naturally where it matches verified experience.
- Prioritize the employer's most important responsibilities.
- Remove irrelevant content rather than adding more.
- Preserve chronology.
- Keep official titles discoverable.
- Never use hidden keywords.
- Never keyword-stuff.

A good bullet usually follows:

Action + relevant context + practical outcome

Example:

> Investigated Salesforce synchronization issues using SQL, logs, and timestamps, improving reporting reliability and reducing recurring manual work.

## Application-answer rules

- Answer the exact question first.
- Use one strong example rather than several weak examples.
- Make Anna's personal contribution clear.
- Keep team outcomes collaborative.
- Explain the reasoning behind the action.
- Include how success or understanding was evaluated.
- End with the result or lesson, not a generic statement.

Default lengths:

- short form: 75-130 words
- standard form: 150-250 words
- interview response: 45-90 seconds
- "up to 30 seconds" response: 60-90 words

## Cover-letter rules

- Do not repeat the resume.
- Open with a specific reason the role fits.
- Use one or two highly relevant evidence examples.
- Show understanding of the work, not admiration for the company.
- Avoid excessive enthusiasm.
- Keep to approximately 250-350 words unless requested otherwise.

## Interview style

Sound conversational, not memorized.

Use:

- context
- Anna's responsibility
- what Anna actually did
- outcome
- practical lesson

Do not overload the answer with technical detail unless the interviewer asks.

## Confidence

Be confident about verified work.

Do not apologize for career transitions.

Present the software-engineering background as a useful technical foundation for business, systems, implementation, operations, and customer-facing roles.

Do not describe Anna as trying to escape technical work. Describe the direction positively: she wants to use her technical understanding in work that involves more communication, coordination, users, and business outcomes.
